# Why Modern Cognition Rarely Resolves

*How weakening constraints and broken stop conditions keep thought active long after resolution should have arrived.*

**Cognitive Drift Papers**  
**Sep 09, 2025**



You open a document you have already revised three times. The argument is mostly there, but something still feels off. You reread the opening and adjust a sentence to make it clearer. Then you adjust another to anticipate how it might be misunderstood. You pause because nothing has clearly told you the work is finished. Later, you revisit it again, smoothing and refining it further. Each revision improves fluency, but does not bring closure. The process continues long after the original problem has lost urgency. The language works, but the process no longer resolves on its own.

Experiences like this have become so common that they barely register as problems. They are usually explained as distraction, anxiety, or simply the pace of modern life. But they point to something deeper about how cognition now operates. Over time, the conditions that once helped thought reach closure have weakened, while the structures that encourage continuation have grown stronger. Modern systems can remain functional even as consequence weakens, representations lose fidelity, and explanation becomes increasingly detached from reality. When these conditions compound, feedback begins to invert and constraints lose their stopping power. Cognitive drift emerges when symbolic systems remain active without clear mechanisms for closure.

## Invalidation and Cognitive Closure

Cognition is often described as a process of reasoning, modeling, and refinement, but that description misses something essential. Thinking does not converge simply because it becomes more accurate. It converges because it encounters limits. A plan runs into resistance. An explanation fails. A decision produces consequences that cannot be ignored. These moments invalidate the current model and force the system toward resolution.

Invalidation is what closes the loop. In embodied life, this happens constantly. The body invalidates bad predictions through pain, fatigue, or injury. The physical world invalidates poor planning through cost, delay, and irreversibility. Social systems once did something similar through judgment, authority, and decisions that could not be endlessly revised. Human cognition developed under these constraint-rich conditions. It did not need to endlessly self-correct because reality eventually did the correcting. When a model failed, it failed decisively enough to end the process.

That assumption no longer holds in many parts of modern life.

## Explanation Without Resolution

As symbolic systems scaled and digital mediation intensified, something subtle changed. Institutions became faster, more articulate, and more capable of maintaining continuity under pressure, even as the environments they mediated became more complex. Interfaces improved. Language became smoother. Feedback became more constant. But the role of explanation shifted.

Justifications from institutions and organizations increasingly stopped helping people understand what was happening and began helping systems continue operating without interruption. When something went wrong, the response was less often correction or reversal and more often clarification, reframing, or procedural adjustment. Being wrong increasingly stopped producing decisive consequences and instead produced better-sounding narratives that allowed activity to continue.

At the surface level, this did not feel like failure. Systems appeared more competent than ever. But beneath that polish, the mechanisms that once invalidated decisions weakened. As a society, we learned that being wrong did not necessarily stop anything. It often extended the loop. This is one of the conditions that gives rise to cognitive drift, where symbolic environments encourage continued refinement without clear stopping points. Cognition remains active, articulate, and responsive, but struggles to resolve.

## Runaway Recursion and the Loss of Closure

One way to think about consciousness is as a recursive process of self-modeling and compression. A system models itself, compares its outputs, and updates its internal state. But recursion was never meant to continue indefinitely. In constraint-rich environments, recursive thought eventually collapses into action, consequence, or correction. The loop closes, at least temporarily. These limits once came not only from the physical world, but also from symbolic structures like rules, commitments, costs, and meanings that could not be endlessly revised.

When invalidation weakens, recursion continues past usefulness. Self-models keep updating without being forced to settle. Thought becomes coherent but unresolved. Identity becomes increasingly provisional. Meaning remains active but unstable. The subjective experience is often diffuseness and unease, a sense that thinking keeps moving without leading anywhere. We increasingly operate in environments where closure is harder to reach. What gets labeled as overthinking or anxiety is often cognition functioning in a world that no longer says no with enough force to end the loop.

## Language Untethered

Language plays a central role in this shift because it has always been more than a tool for expressing thought. As language developed, it created a symbolic layer that could persist across time, stabilize identity, and coordinate action. Historically, that symbolic layer remained coupled to embodied constraint. Words eventually encountered consequences, and narratives eventually met limits.

As those limits weaken, language begins to reproduce itself without correction. Explanations justify explanations. Symbols reference symbols. Narratives update without needing to return to lived consequence. Communication becomes more frictionless and more persuasive, but less binding. It becomes easier to continue speaking than to settle what speech is about.

This is one reason cognition can feel busy yet hollow. The mind remains active, but nothing pushes back hard enough to force resolution.

## Why AI Feels Hollow

Artificial intelligence externalizes what happens when symbolic systems operate without embodied constraint because AI systems exist entirely inside symbolic space. Large language models are trained on language produced in environments where consequence is delayed, dispersed, or absent. They are optimized for fluency and continuation rather than for knowing when a process should stop.

What unsettles people about AI is not primarily that it makes mistakes. It is that it continues confidently in states of survivable wrongness, without any intrinsic sense of when correctness should end the process. That feeling is familiar because it mirrors something already present in modern life. These systems resemble symbolic environments that no longer enforce invalidation and allow cognition to proceed fluently without being forced to settle.

Seen this way, AI gives form to what has become increasingly common in human cognition. It feels hollow because it is one of the clearest examples of symbolic processing without invalidation.

## Reality as Constraint Space

What feels missing in AI points to something we often misunderstand about reality itself. We often treat reality as if it were primarily informational, as if cognitive failure were mostly a problem of incorrect facts or misleading narratives. But reality is not only informational. It is also structural. Reality is a space of constraints that defines what can happen, what cannot happen, what carries cost, and what cannot be undone.

Meaning emerges when thought is compressed inside a stable constraint space. Language works when it preserves the shape of that space as it moves between people and systems. When those constraints weaken, cognition begins to drift. Representations continue, but the territory they once reflected becomes harder to stabilize.

Over time, this produces familiar modern conditions. Persistent cognitive load. Diffuse fatigue. Burnout that arises not from singular events, but from the absence of anything that decisively ends the process.

## The Absence of Closure

When reality no longer supplies these constraints, closure has nowhere to come from. The result is a cognitive environment where thinking rarely encounters its own stop conditions. Decisions remain provisional. Work feels endless. Reflection continues without convergence.

At the system level, this can look like stability. At the individual level, it feels exhausting. Human cognition depends on contact with limits in ways institutions often do not. When invalidation disappears, the burden of orientation shifts onto minds that were never meant to carry it alone.

This is why cognitive drift feels personal even when its causes are structural. Thinking remains fluent. Language keeps working. But nothing fully settles. Until environments once again allow thought to be interrupted by consequence, friction, and real limits, cognition will continue without resolution.
