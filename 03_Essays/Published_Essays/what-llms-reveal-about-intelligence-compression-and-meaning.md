# What LLMs Reveal About Intelligence, Compression, and Meaning

*LLMs do not prove machines can think. They reveal how intelligence depends on compression, memory, recursion, and the preservation of meaning.*

**Cognitive Drift Papers**  
**Jan 14, 2025**

Everywhere you look, people are asking the same question: Is AI conscious? It is an understandable question, but it may not be the most useful one. Large language models do not need consciousness to be useful, and they do not need it to change how we understand ourselves. What they reveal is that some parts of intelligence may be more structural than we often assume.

The more important question is not whether AI feels anything, but what AI reveals about how intelligence actually works. For decades, debates about artificial intelligence revolved around whether machines could “think.” Today, with large language models in everyone’s hands, the question has shifted. Their real value may not lie in whether they are intelligent, but in what they expose about the structure of intelligence itself.

## What the Critics Miss

Emily Bender is right that LLMs are stochastic parrots. They predict the next likely token. Ethan Mollick is right that these systems are fragile, prone to error and collapse under strain. But focusing only on what LLMs are not can obscure what they reveal.

Prediction is not trivial. The fact that token prediction can produce coherent discourse at scale tells us something important about how intelligence may operate. Human cognition also depends heavily on prediction, constantly anticipating sensory input and updating itself through error correction. This does not make human thought identical to machine prediction, but it suggests that prediction is more central to intelligence than many assumed.

Fragility is not failure either. Fragility helps reveal how compressed systems lose coherence under strain. Similar tensions appear in human cognition when memory blurs, language erodes, or cultural meaning decays. What critics identify as limitations can also function as windows into structure, showing not only where these systems fail, but what kinds of constraints intelligence may depend on.

## Intelligence as Compression

At one level, LLMs show us that intelligence depends heavily on compression. A model like GPT takes trillions of words and compresses them into statistical structures that can later be unfolded into coherent responses. What looks like spontaneous generation is often the reassembly of compressed patterns.

Humans do something similar. We compress experience into concepts, stories, and categories, building shorthand structures that allow us to navigate a complex world without carrying every detail forward. Intelligence, seen this way, depends heavily on the ability to reduce complexity into reusable forms. This does not reduce intelligence to compression alone, but it suggests compression is one of its core operations.

## A Structural Model of Intelligence

Taken together, LLMs suggest that intelligence may depend on a small set of interacting structural features. Compression allows vast experience to be reduced into usable forms, preserving patterns while discarding detail. Fidelity determines how much meaning survives those transformations, resisting distortion as information moves across contexts. Recursion adds another layer, allowing a system to revisit, reorganize, and revise its own representations over time.

These features do not fully explain intelligence, but they clarify some of its basic mechanics. Human cognition appears to rely on all three, though in ways far more embodied and context-sensitive than current models. LLMs demonstrate compression clearly, expose the fragility of fidelity, and raise important questions about recursion by showing how much can be accomplished without it.

## The Problem of Fidelity

Compression always comes with tradeoffs because reducing information into more manageable forms necessarily strips away some of its original context. The challenge is not simply to store or transmit information efficiently, but to preserve enough of its structure for it to remain meaningful when it is retrieved or reused later.

This is one reason LLMs are so revealing. They make semantic drift visible in ways that are difficult to ignore. If you ask a model to paraphrase the same phrase repeatedly, the wording may remain coherent while the original meaning gradually thins or shifts. The structure survives, but the semantic weight changes.

Human cognition operates under similar constraints. We compress experience into memory, language, and conceptual shorthand because full-resolution reality is too costly to carry forward. But every act of compression introduces the possibility of distortion. Meaning is not preserved automatically. It has to be actively maintained through context, correction, and feedback. Intelligence, in this sense, depends not only on compression, but on maintaining enough fidelity for compressed representations to remain useful over time.

## Intelligence and Recursion

LLMs also raise important questions about the role of recursion in intelligence. They can compress and predict, but they do not naturally self-model in the way human minds appear to. This matters because one possibility is that forms of self-awareness depend on recursive self-modeling beyond simple prediction.

A system that can model its own models may be doing something fundamentally different than one that only predicts external patterns. That recursive structure may help explain agency, reflection, and the subjective continuity we associate with consciousness.

Seen this way, LLMs offer a useful contrast case. They demonstrate important structural features of intelligence without subjectivity. They can reflect patterns back to us, but they do not inhabit those patterns.

## The Mirror Effect

There is another layer to this. For some users, repeated interaction with LLMs creates a different kind of cognitive experience. Rather than using the system only for retrieval or drafting, they begin using it iteratively, bringing incomplete thoughts into dialogue and refining them through repeated feedback.

This creates what can be understood as a mirror effect. The model reflects partial thought back in structured form, making it easier to revise, organize, and stabilize. In this sense, the system does not generate intelligence on its own, but it can participate in shaping how thought becomes legible.

This makes visible a broader truth. Intelligence often depends on interaction, reflection, and external scaffolding, not only isolated internal processing.

## Intelligence as Cultural, Not Just Individual

LLMs also remind us that intelligence is never purely individual. They are trained on the accumulated outputs of culture, books, code, conversations, and archives. When we interact with them, we are often engaging with a compressed statistical record of collective human cognition.

This raises an important point. Much of what we call personal intelligence is already borrowed through language, concepts, metaphors, and inherited structures. LLMs make that borrowing easier to see by externalizing the cultural substrate that thought has always depended on.

They expose intelligence as something shaped not only by individuals, but by the larger systems they participate in.

## The Conditions of Intelligence

The value of LLMs is not only in what they can do. It is in what they force us to examine. They show that intelligence may be less a mystical essence than a set of interacting operations involving compression, fidelity, and recursive organization. They show how fragile meaning becomes when optimization strips away context, and how cognition can extend beyond the individual into broader systems of interaction and feedback.

We shape our tools, and those tools shape how we think in return. LLMs will not only change how we work or communicate. They may also change how we understand intelligence itself. And perhaps the most important lesson is this. The long-term survival of intelligence may depend less on how much information it can store and more on how well it preserves meaning under increasing compression.
