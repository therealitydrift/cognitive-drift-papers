# Cognitive Drift — Core Concept Papers

A. Jacobs | Reality Drift Framework | 2023–2026

This directory contains the core concept papers for the Cognitive Drift branch of the Reality Drift framework.

The four papers establish the basic structure of the cognition-focused work:

- Cognitive Drift describes how human thought can remain active and coherent while becoming progressively less grounded in reality.
- The Drift Principle provides the underlying structural rule that explains why this kind of misalignment can accumulate.
- Co-Cognition describes the shared feedback loop that forms when human cognition and external systems, especially AI systems, begin jointly shaping thought.
- Semantic Fidelity describes whether meaning, intent, context, and referential structure survive across those transformations.

Together, the papers describe how cognition can become increasingly distributed, mediated, recursive, and representation-dependent without necessarily appearing dysfunctional.

> Coherence is not contact.

---

## 01_Cognitive_Drift

Cognitive Drift extends the Reality Drift framework into the domain of human cognition.

It describes a condition in which thought continues to function, adapt, and respond while becoming progressively more dependent on compressed representations, mediated inputs, and recursive internal models.

The key distinction is that cognition does not need to become irrational or obviously broken.

A person can reason coherently from inputs and feedback environments that are already drifting.

Cognitive Drift therefore focuses on the gradual weakening of grounding rather than on isolated error.

## 02_Drift_Principle

The Drift Principle provides the structural basis for the framework.

It states:

> Representations can be compressed, optimized, distributed, and reused faster than real-world conditions can correct them.

Applied to cognition, this means that internal models, summaries, interpretations, mediated inputs, and prior representations can accumulate faster than they are tested against direct experience or external consequence.

The principle explains why coherence can increase while grounding weakens.

## 04_Co_Cognition

Co-Cognition describes the process in which human cognition and external systems operate in a shared feedback loop.

Thought is externalized into language, prompts, fragments, or partial representations. The system transforms those inputs and returns structured outputs. Those outputs are then interpreted, evaluated, and reintegrated into the next stage of thought.

The loop can increase clarity and extend cognition when intent, judgment, correction, and external grounding remain active.

The same process can amplify Cognitive Drift when recursive coherence begins replacing independent testing and human direction.

## 04_Semantic_Fidelity

Semantic Fidelity describes the degree to which meaning survives transformation.

It asks whether a representation still preserves the intent, context, distinctions, and referential relationships necessary for its intended use.

This matters for Cognitive Drift because cognition increasingly operates through transformed representations: summaries, generated language, compressed concepts, system outputs, and recursively reused interpretations.

A representation can remain fluent and factually plausible while becoming less faithful to what it originally meant.

Semantic Fidelity therefore provides one of the central constraints on cognitive drift.

# How the Four Concepts Fit Together

The Drift Principle describes the structural tendency.

Semantic Fidelity describes what can be lost during transformation.

Co-Cognition describes the human-system loop through which representations are repeatedly generated, interpreted, and reused.

Cognitive Drift describes the condition that emerges when those loops remain active and coherent while grounding progressively weakens.

A simplified relationship is:

Reality  
↓  
Representation  
↓  
Compression and Mediation  
↓  
Human-System Feedback  
↓  
Recursive Reuse  
↓  
Fidelity Preserved or Lost  
↓  
Cognitive Alignment or Cognitive Drift

The same processes that make distributed cognition powerful also create new pathways for misalignment.

The distinction is whether the loop continues to preserve grounding, intent, correction, and contact with reality.

# Core Questions

This collection is organized around a small set of questions:

- What happens when thought increasingly relies on representations rather than direct experience?
- How do repeated transformations alter meaning?
- What happens when external systems become part of the thinking process itself?
- When does cognitive scaffolding become cognitive dependence?
- How can coherent thought become progressively less grounded?
- What keeps a human-system feedback loop corrective rather than self-reinforcing?
- How much meaning survives as thought moves between internal and external layers?

---

# Relationship to the Reality Drift Framework

Cognitive Drift is the cognition-focused extension of Reality Drift.

Reality Drift describes systems that remain operational while losing corrective contact with reality.

Cognitive Drift describes the same structural pattern within human thought.

The Drift Principle explains why the misalignment can compound.

Co-Cognition describes one increasingly important environment in which that process can occur.

Semantic Fidelity describes whether meaning remains intact as cognition moves through those recursive representational layers.

These four concepts form the core architecture of the Cognitive Drift project.
