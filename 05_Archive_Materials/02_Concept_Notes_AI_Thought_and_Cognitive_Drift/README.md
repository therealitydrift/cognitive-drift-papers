# AI, Thought, and Cognitive Drift

This collection examines how artificial intelligence changes memory, reasoning, attention, inquiry, knowledge work, and the structure of thought itself.

The papers connect AI assistance, cognitive offloading, external memory, information overload, confidence, dependency, and human–AI collaboration to the broader concepts of **Co-Cognition**, **Cognitive Drift**, **Epistemic Drift**, **Extended Cognition**, and **Cognitive Artifacts**.

The central pattern is that AI does not merely help people complete existing cognitive tasks. As it becomes embedded in writing, searching, remembering, questioning, and decision-making, it begins reorganizing which mental operations people perform themselves, which ones they delegate, and what thinking comes to feel like.
